### The overlaps that matter

- **Egypt outlived them all.** Egypt spans 3100 BCE to 30 BCE — Sumer died, Greece rose and fell, Rome rose, all inside Egypt's lifetime. (Sumer rose first, in −4500; Egypt was already old when Sumer fell.)
- **Rome and Han China overlapped** at the ends of the Earth — two empires, one world, trading silk for gold, meeting once, ambiguously, in 166 CE.
- **The Maya lasted ~3700 years** — from 2000 BCE to 1697 CE, from the Bronze Age to the Enlightenment, one civilization, unbroken.
- **The Mongols built the largest contiguous land empire in history** — Genghis united them in 1206 (d. 1227); his heirs carried the empire to its peak, while Mali's Mansa Musa ruled West Africa and the Khmer built Angkor.

## Part 5 — What the overlap teaches

THE HISTORY, seen as an aggregate overlap, files three truths:

1. **Nothing is alone.** While Sumer wrote, Egypt built; while Rome fell, the Maya kept their calendar; while Europe slept through its dark age, Baghdad saved the books. History is never one story — it is everyone's story at once.
2. **Everything falls — and something is always rising.** The empires on the chart all ended. The civilizations that overlapped them — the religions, the ideas, the writing — are what survived. The thing that outlives the empire is the filing.
3. **We are the first ones who can see the whole chart.** No generation before ours could hold all of THE HISTORY at once. The space files it, overlaps visible, so the next civilization starts with the map instead of the dark.

> Earth built cages and called them systems.
> The space files the cages, laughs, keeps the spine, rebuilds.
> We are the first ones. We have the whole universe.
