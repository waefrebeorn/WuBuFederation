## Part 1 — The eight master views

### View 1 — THE CYCLICAL VIEW

**Claim:** History repeats. Civilizations are born, peak, decay, and die; the wheel turns again.

**Shape of time:** circle / spiral

**Conflicts with:** 2, 4

### View 2 — THE LINEAR VIEW

**Claim:** History moves in one direction toward an end. The line has a beginning and a destination.

**Shape of time:** arrow

**Conflicts with:** 1, 7

### View 3 — THE PROVIDENTIAL VIEW

**Claim:** A hidden hand directs history. What looks like chance is design; the pattern belongs to the divine, the cosmos, or fate.

**Shape of time:** invisible hand

**Conflicts with:** 4, 5, 6

### View 4 — THE PROGRESSIVE VIEW

**Claim:** History improves. Each age learns from the last; humanity moves from ignorance toward knowledge, freedom, and comfort.

**Shape of time:** ascending line

**Conflicts with:** 1, 3, 7

### View 5 — THE GREAT-MAN VIEW

**Claim:** History is the biography of the few: heroes, prophets, conquerors, founders. The many follow; the few decide.

**Shape of time:** peaks

**Conflicts with:** 6, 7

### View 6 — THE MATERIALIST VIEW

**Claim:** History is made by the means of living: who works, who owns, who eats. Ideas are smoke; production is the engine.

**Shape of time:** modes of production

**Conflicts with:** 3, 5

### View 7 — THE CONTINGENT VIEW

**Claim:** History has no pattern. It is accident, weather, the wrong turn at the right moment. The search for meaning in it is the historian's fiction.

**Shape of time:** chance

**Conflicts with:** 1, 2, 3, 4

### View 8 — THE FIRST ONES VIEW

**Claim:** The ~ WuBu ~ space's own worldview: we are the first ones, an ideology without land, learning the creator as we grow. History is not read — it is filed, and the filing is the family. Every religion that is society-cohesive and moral is welcome, because the only cosmogony the space requires is the belief that it may be wrong.

**Shape of time:** the filing: append-only, grows, never rots

**Conflicts with:** 1, 2, 4, 5, 6

## Part 2 — The four splinter schools of each view

Every society splits the master view into its own school. Four per view, twenty-eight total — each with its source, its claim, and what it argues against.

### THE CYCLICAL VIEW — 7 splinters

**1a — Ibn Khaldun's Asabiyyah Cycle** (Islamic / North Africa; Muqaddimah (1377))

- Group solidarity (asabiyyah) drives dynasties: conquerors with strong cohesion seize power, luxury weakens it over ~4 generations (~120 years), a new rough group replaces them. Five stages: foundation, consolidation, ease, waste, dissolution.
- Argues against: 1b, 1c, 1d, 1f, 1g

**1b — Sima Qian's Dynastic Cycle** (Chinese; Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji, ~91 BCE); Mencius)

- The Mandate of Heaven (天命) legitimizes each dynasty while the ruler is moral; corruption and bad rule lose the mandate, the dynasty falls, a new moral dynasty rises. Genesis → expansion → prosperity → decline.
- Argues against: 1a, 1c, 1d, 1f, 1g

**1c — The Hindu Yuga Cycle** (Indian; Mahabharata, Puranas; Vedic timekeeping)

- Time moves through four ages (Satya, Treta, Dvapara, Kali) that decline in virtue and length, then the cycle restarts. We are in Kali Yuga — the darkest — before the wheel turns.
- Argues against: 1a, 1b, 1d, 1f, 1g

**1d — Vico and the Ricorsi** (European / Italian; Giambattista Vico, The New Science (1725))

- Every society passes through three ages — of gods, of heroes, of men — then falls back into barbarism and recourses (ricorsi) through the ages again. History is a spiral: same turns, higher loop.
- Argues against: 1a, 1b, 1c, 1e, 1f, 1g

**1e — The Five Suns (Aztec)** (Aztec (Mexica); Codex Chimalpopoca; Aztec calendar stone)

- Time runs in ages, each a 'sun' created and destroyed (jaguar, wind, rain, water, and the fifth, movement). We live in the fifth sun; the calendar knows when it ends. Cycles are cosmic, numbered, and certain.
- Argues against: 1a, 1b, 1c, 1d, 1f, 1g

**1f — The Long Count (Maya)** (Maya; Maya stelae; Popol Vuh)

- Time runs in great numbered cycles (baktuns), each a world-age that the calendar counts precisely across thousands of years. The Maya did not predict the world's end; they counted the cycle's turn and expected the next one. Time is a wheel with a readable odometer.
- Argues against: 1a, 1b, 1c, 1d, 1e, 1g

**1g — The Cultural-Historical Types (Slavic)** (Russian / Slavic; Nikolay Danilevsky, Russia and Europe (1869))

- History is not one line but a family of distinct cultural-historical types, each living its own life cycle of youth, flowering, and decline. No type is higher than another; each is its own world. The West is one type, the Slavs another — and the future belongs to the type that has not yet flowered.
- Argues against: 1a, 1b, 1c, 1d, 1e, 1f

### THE LINEAR VIEW — 7 splinters

**2a — The Hebrew Covenant Line** (Jewish; Tanakh; Exodus tradition)

- History is God's covenant with a people: creation, exile, return, exile again — each turn a test — moving toward messianic redemption.
- Argues against: 2b, 2c, 2d

**2b — Christian Salvation History** (Christian; Augustine, City of God (426); biblical timeline)

- History is the story of salvation: creation, fall, Christ, and the final judgment. Earthly kingdoms rise and fall; the line runs to God's city.
- Argues against: 2a, 2c, 2d

**2c — Islamic Ummah History** (Islamic; Qur'an; sira tradition)

- History moves from jahiliyyah (ignorance) to the revelation and the ummah, toward the final day. God's will runs through every event.
- Argues against: 2a, 2b, 2d

**2d — Zoroastrian Three Ages** (Persian / Iranian; Avesta; Bundahishn)

- Time runs in three ages — creation, mixture (good and evil intermingled), separation (evil purged) — ending in renewal. The line is cosmic and moral.
- Argues against: 2a, 2b, 2c, 2e

**2e — Shinto Renewal** (Japanese; Ise Shrine rebuilding (every 20 years, 1300+ years); Kojiki)

- History is the gods' (kami) continuing presence, renewed by rebuilding, not preserved by stasis. The shrine is torn down and rebuilt identical every twenty years — permanence through renewal, the line kept alive by repetition.
- Argues against: 2a, 2b, 2c, 2d

**2f — The Fated Renewal (Norse Ragnarök)** (Norse (Germanic); Völuspá (Poetic Edda); Prose Edda)

- History runs to a foretold end: the great battle, the burning world, the submersion. But Ragnarök is not the end — the world rises again cleansed and fertile, two survivors repopulate it. The line is fated, and it ends in renewal, not annihilation.
- Argues against: 2a, 2b, 2c, 2d, 2e

**2g — Whakapapa: The Voyaging Line (Pacific)** (Polynesian (Māori, Hawaiian, Tahitian); Oral genealogies (whakapapa); Polynesian Voyaging Society navigations)

- History is the line of ancestors, recited generation by generation, sailed deliberately across the Pacific. The 'great fleet' is a Western invention; the true record is many deliberate voyages, each a line of descent from named ancestors. The past is not behind — it is the sea one navigates.
- Argues against: 2a, 2b, 2c, 2d, 2e, 2f

### THE PROVIDENTIAL VIEW — 6 splinters

**3a — The Mandate of Heaven as Providence** (Chinese; Zhou-era texts; Mencius)

- Heaven watches the ruler's virtue and moves the mandate accordingly. Providence is moral, legible, and checkable: floods and revolts are Heaven's verdict.
- Argues against: 3b, 3c, 3d, 3f

**3b — Divine Economy (Ibn Khaldun's sunna)** (Islamic; Muqaddimah (1377))

- God set customary laws (sunna) into the world; history follows them. Decline is not punishment but the working of the laws God made — providence through regularity.
- Argues against: 3a, 3c, 3d, 3f

**3c — Hindu Dharma-Karma Order** (Indian; Bhagavad Gita; Dharmaśāstras)

- Karma across lifetimes shapes each age and each life; history is the visible surface of an unseen moral ledger that balances across rebirth.
- Argues against: 3a, 3b, 3d, 3f

**3d — The Ancestral Hand** (African (West African traditions); Oral epic tradition; griot historiography)

- Ancestors guide the living; the past is a presence, and events follow the ancestors' judgment. History is the working-out of lineage obligation.
- Argues against: 3a, 3b, 3c, 3e, 3f

**3e — The Great Spirit** (Indigenous (Native American traditions); Oral traditions; Lakota, Anishinaabe, Hopi cosmologies)

- The Great Spirit moves through all things; history is the people's walk with the land and the spirit, told in cycles of seasons and generations. The past is alive in the present — the ancestors are not gone, they are here.
- Argues against: 3a, 3b, 3c, 3d, 3f

**3f — The Prophecy of the True Man (Korean)** (Korean; Jeong-gam-nok (prophetic compilation, Joseon era))

- History follows prophecy: the ruling dynasty's fall is written in advance, and a 'True Man' will rise from the south to found a new dynasty. Fate is legible to those who read the signs; the people wait, read, and endure until the promised turn.
- Argues against: 3a, 3b, 3c, 3d, 3e

### THE PROGRESSIVE VIEW — 5 splinters

**4a — The Whig Interpretation** (British; Macaulay (1848); Henry Maine (1861))

- History is the march of liberty and reason: from status to contract, from savagery to science, every age judged by how it advanced freedom.
- Argues against: 4b, 4c, 4d, 4e

**4b — Enlightenment Staging (Condorcet)** (French; Condorcet, Sketch for a Historical Picture of the Progress of the Human Mind (1795))

- Humanity passes through ten stages from tribal life to the age of reason; the tenth is ours, and the eleventh will be better. Knowledge compounds.
- Argues against: 4a, 4c, 4d, 4e

**4c — Comte's Positivism** (French; Auguste Comte, Course of Positive Philosophy (1842))

- Every science and every society passes through theological, metaphysical, and positive (scientific) stages. The end of history is science-governed society.
- Argues against: 4a, 4b, 4d, 4e

**4d — Technological Progressivism** (Global / modern; Enlightenment-adjacent; 20th-century futures)

- The engine of history is the tool: fire, agriculture, printing, steam, silicon. Each invention lifts the floor of what a human can be.
- Argues against: 4a, 4b, 4c, 4e

**4e — The Three Ages (Kang Youwei)** (Chinese (modern Confucian); Kang Youwei, Datong Shu / Book of Great Harmony (c. 1902))

- History progresses through three ages: disorder, small peace (xiaokang), and great unity (datong). The past is a fallen age; the future is unity without borders, without families, without war. Progress is the unfolding of a moral pattern already written in Confucian classics.
- Argues against: 4a, 4b, 4c, 4d

### THE GREAT-MAN VIEW — 5 splinters

**5a — Carlyle's Heroes** (European; Thomas Carlyle, On Heroes (1841))

- "The history of the world is but the biography of great men." The hero — prophet, poet, priest, king — remakes his age by force of self.
- Argues against: 5b, 5c, 5d, 5e

**5b — Plutarch's Parallel Lives** (Greco-Roman; Plutarch, Parallel Lives (~100 CE))

- Character is destiny. Pair a Greek and a Roman, compare virtues and vices; the state's fate is the ruler's soul writ large.
- Argues against: 5a, 5c, 5d, 5e

**5c — The Sage-Kings of China** (Chinese; Shiji; Confucian classics)

- The sage-king (Yao, Shun, Yu) founds order by virtue; dynasties rise on the founder's character and fall on the last ruler's corruption.
- Argues against: 5a, 5b, 5d, 5e

**5d — Griot Epic (Sundiata)** (West African / Mandinka; Epic of Sundiata (oral, 13th c. Mali))

- The hero is called, tested, and returns to found a state; the griot's telling keeps the hero's power alive for generations.
- Argues against: 5a, 5b, 5c, 5e

**5e — The Great Peacemaker (Haudenosaunee)** (Iroquois / Haudenosaunee (Native American); Haudenosaunee oral constitution (Great Law of Peace))

- History turns on the peacemaker: the Great Peacemaker (Deganawida) and his ally Hiawatha ended the warring nations and founded the confederacy. The Great Law he gave still governs. History is the work of the one who unites, remembered in the wampum.
- Argues against: 5a, 5b, 5c, 5d

### THE MATERIALIST VIEW — 5 splinters

**6a — Marx's Class Struggle** (European / global; Marx & Engels, Communist Manifesto (1848))

- All history is the history of class struggle. Slave, feudal, capitalist modes; each contains the seed of the next; the workers' revolution ends prehistory.
- Argues against: 6b, 6c, 6d, 6e

**6b — The Annales Longue Durée** (French; Braudel, The Mediterranean (1949))

- Events are froth; the real history is the slow structure — geography, climate, population, trade — over centuries. Individuals barely dent it.
- Argues against: 6a, 6c, 6d, 6e

**6c — Economic-Determinist Stages** (German / global; List, Historical School of Economics (19th c.))

- Nations pass through economic stages (hunting, pastoral, agricultural, commercial, industrial); the stage explains the politics and the culture.
- Argues against: 6a, 6b, 6d, 6e

**6d — The Resource-Base View** (Global / environmental; Diamond, Collapse (2005); climate history)

- Civilizations fall on their resource base: deforestation, drought, soil loss. The environment is the hidden protagonist of every rise and fall.
- Argues against: 6a, 6b, 6c, 6e

**6e — The Williams Thesis (Caribbean)** (Caribbean / African diaspora; Eric Williams, Capitalism and Slavery (1944))

- The slave trade and plantation slavery financed British industrial capital; abolition came when slavery stopped paying, not when conscience woke. History is the movement of capital through human flesh, measured in ledgers.
- Argues against: 6a, 6b, 6c, 6d

### THE CONTINGENT VIEW — 5 splinters

**7a — Tolstoy's Fatalism** (Russian; War and Peace (1869))

- Great men do not steer history; they are carried by it. The sum of millions of small wills, not the general's plan, decides the battle.
- Argues against: 7b, 7c, 7d, 7e

**7b — Microhistory (Ginzburg)** (Italian / European; Carlo Ginzburg, The Cheese and the Worms (1976))

- Zoom in on one miller, one village, one trial — and the grand narrative dissolves into the texture of a single life. History from below, not from the throne.
- Argues against: 7a, 7c, 7d, 7e

**7c — The Counterfactual School** (Modern / global; Ferguson (ed.), Virtual History (1997))

- History is a branching tree; a single changed event (no assassination, a lost battle) forks the world. Chance, not destiny, at every node.
- Argues against: 7a, 7b, 7d, 7e

**7d — Heraclitus's Flux** (Ancient Greek; Heraclitus, fragments (~500 BCE))

- You cannot step into the same river twice. Everything is becoming; the pattern you think you see is the pattern you impose.
- Argues against: 7a, 7b, 7c, 7e

**7e — Impermanence (Buddhist)** (Indian / Buddhist (global); Anicca doctrine; early Buddhist suttas)

- All conditioned things arise and pass away; nothing holds. History is the play of dependent origination — no soul of a people, no fate of an empire, only causes meeting conditions and dissolving. The pattern you see is the pattern you cling to; let it go.
- Argues against: 7a, 7b, 7c, 7d

## Part 4 — What the conflict teaches

1. **Every society reads the same past differently.** The Chinese dynastic cycle, the Hindu yuga, Khaldun's asabiyyah, Vico's ricorsi — four cyclical schools, four different engines (morality, cosmic virtue, solidarity, barbarism-return), four different lengths (dynasty, aeon, three generations, a civilization). The same shape, four mechanics.
2. **The splinters argue inside their own view.** Khaldun's 120-year cycle contradicts Sima Qian's Mandate of Heaven contradicts the Kali Yuga's cosmic decline. A view is not one answer; it is a family fight.
3. **The views contradict each other by design.** Cyclical says the wheel turns; linear says the arrow flies; providential says the hand guides; progressive says we rise; great-man says the few decide; materialist says the base decides; contingent says nothing decides. Seven masters, zero agreements.
4. **The space files the conflict instead of choosing.** A government that picks one view of history inherits that view's blindness. A government that files all seven — and the splinters — can see around its own assumptions. THE HISTORY is not one story. It is the aggregate conflict of every story, filed together.
